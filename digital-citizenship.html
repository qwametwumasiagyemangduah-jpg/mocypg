Ministry programme

Responsible Digital Citizenship & Educational Technology Use

A six-month awareness and engagement programme promoting responsible social media behaviour and the productive use of government-provided tablets among students in Ghana.

Duration: Six monthsPilot: Selected schools and communities
← Back to Publications
Programme overview

Digital technology and social media are important to communication, education and access to information. Government-supported digital learning initiatives, including the provision of tablets to students, create valuable opportunities to strengthen learning and digital participation. These opportunities are most effective when students are equipped to use technology safely, responsibly and productively.

This programme promotes responsible social media use and encourages students to make educational use of digital devices. It also addresses cyberbullying, online insults, misinformation, impersonation, unauthorised recording and the sharing of photographs or videos without consent.

The programme will be implemented through a six-month pilot in selected schools and communities. Activities, participating schools, institutional responsibilities and resource arrangements will be coordinated with relevant stakeholders.

The programme emphasises education, guidance, privacy, respect and practical digital citizenship. It is not intended to discourage digital learning or restrict lawful expression.

1. Background and key issues

Digital tools can support research, classroom learning, communication and personal development. Their educational value may be reduced when devices are used mainly for unrelated entertainment or when social media activity disrupts learning. Online conduct can also affect the safety, dignity and privacy of students, teachers and other community members.

  • Misuse of educational tablets: use of devices for activities unrelated to learning rather than their educational purpose.
  • Digital distractions: social media and entertainment use during learning hours.
  • Cyberbullying and online insults: insulting, threatening or humiliating others through digital platforms.
  • Unauthorised recording and livestreaming: recording teachers, classmates or classroom activities without permission.
  • Misinformation: sharing unverified claims, rumours or misleading videos.
  • Sharing images without consent: circulating photographs or videos of others without permission.
  • Online scams and impersonation: fake accounts and deceptive messages used to obtain personal information or money.

2. Programme goal and objectives

Overall goal: To promote a generation of young people who are digitally capable and use technology in a responsible, respectful and informed manner.

  • Promote responsible social media use among students and young people.
  • Encourage educational use of government-provided tablets and other digital learning devices.
  • Reduce avoidable digital distractions during lessons.
  • Improve understanding of cyberbullying, online privacy, misinformation and digital safety.
  • Promote respect for teachers, classmates and other social media users.
  • Encourage parents and teachers to guide students on responsible digital behaviour.
  • Strengthen cooperation among relevant institutions and education stakeholders.

3. Proposed programme components

3.1 Digital responsibility education

Deliver school-based awareness sessions on digital citizenship, online safety, respectful communication, privacy and responsible social media use.

3.2 Guidance on government-provided tablets

Encourage relevant authorities and education stakeholders to provide clear guidance on the proper use, care and educational purpose of government-provided tablets.

3.3 Responsible device use in schools

Support schools to establish clear and fair expectations for phone and tablet use during lessons. Recording or livestreaming without permission should be addressed through appropriate school guidance.

3.4 Cyberbullying and online harm awareness

Explain the effects of online bullying, insults, threats and harassment, and provide information on safe ways to report concerns.

3.5 Parental and community engagement

Encourage parents, guardians and community stakeholders to guide young people in their use of digital devices and online platforms.

3.6 Reporting and institutional cooperation

Promote safe reporting of online abuse, scams and privacy concerns. Matters requiring formal intervention should be referred to appropriate authorities.

4. Six-month implementation roadmap

The programme will be delivered as a six-month pilot in selected schools and communities. The detailed schedule, participating schools, responsibilities and resources will be coordinated with relevant stakeholders.

Month 1 — Research and stakeholder consultation

  • Engage students, teachers, parents and school authorities.
  • Identify common challenges relating to tablet use and social media misconduct.
  • Consult relevant institutions and digital experts.
  • Review existing laws, school rules and digital education policies.

Expected result: A clearer understanding of the issues, stakeholder priorities and recommendations.

Month 2 — Education and awareness preparation

  • Develop accessible educational materials on digital responsibility.
  • Prepare awareness messages, posters and social media content.
  • Prepare guidance on responsible tablet use.
  • Develop presentations and session materials for selected schools.

Expected result: Educational materials and awareness activities prepared for the pilot.

Month 3 — School awareness programmes

  • Begin awareness programmes in selected schools.
  • Discuss the educational purpose and responsible use of government-provided tablets.
  • Explain how social media distractions can affect learning.
  • Educate students on cyberbullying, online privacy and respectful communication.

Expected result: Improved awareness among participating students.

Month 4 — Community and parental engagement

  • Organise engagement sessions with parents and teachers.
  • Encourage parents to guide their children's use of digital devices.
  • Promote responsible social media messages in communities.
  • Encourage schools to strengthen clear and fair device-use guidelines.

Expected result: Greater involvement of parents, teachers and communities.

Month 5 — Monitoring and evaluation

  • Assess the activities carried out during the pilot.
  • Collect feedback from students, teachers and parents.
  • Identify challenges affecting implementation.
  • Review whether participants demonstrate improved understanding of responsible digital behaviour.

Expected result: A progress report identifying achievements, challenges and areas for improvement.

Month 6 — Final review and recommendations

  • Prepare a report on the six-month programme.
  • Present findings and recommendations to relevant stakeholders.
  • Identify activities that could be continued.
  • Recommend whether and how the programme could be expanded.

Expected result: A final report with practical recommendations for possible wider implementation.

6. Monitoring and measurement of success

  • Number of schools and communities reached.
  • Number of students, teachers and parents participating.
  • Changes in students' understanding of responsible tablet and social media use.
  • Feedback from participating schools and communities.
  • Number of awareness activities organised.
  • Recommendations received from relevant stakeholders.
  • Feasibility of continuing or expanding the programme.

Monitoring should focus on learning, participation and practical outcomes. Any data collection should be proportionate and handled with appropriate regard for privacy.

7. Guiding principles and safeguards

  • Respect for students' rights: Students should be educated and guided rather than unfairly punished. Any disciplinary measures should be reasonable and consistent with established school rules.
  • Protection of privacy: Students, teachers and other individuals should be protected against unauthorised recording and sharing of private information.
  • Freedom of expression: The programme should not prevent students or other citizens from expressing lawful opinions.
  • Responsible use of technology: The objective is to encourage productive, safe and responsible use of technology, not to discourage digital learning.
  • Stakeholder coordination: Activities and institutional responsibilities should be coordinated collaboratively.

8. Expected outcomes

  • Greater awareness of responsible social media use among participating students.
  • Improved understanding of the educational purpose of government-provided tablets.
  • Better awareness of cyberbullying, misinformation, privacy and online scams.
  • More constructive engagement among students, parents, teachers and schools on digital behaviour.
  • Practical recommendations to inform future digital responsibility and awareness initiatives.

9. Conclusion and way forward

Digital technology and social media offer important opportunities for education, communication and national development. Students should be supported with the knowledge and guidance needed to use digital tools for learning and personal development.

The programme will be implemented through a six-month pilot in selected schools and communities. Relevant stakeholders will be engaged to coordinate activities, responsibilities and required resources. Youth Parliament Ghana will contribute to stakeholder engagement, awareness activities and the development of practical recommendations.

The intended outcome is to promote young people who are not only digitally skilled, but also responsible, respectful and conscious of the impact of their actions online.
